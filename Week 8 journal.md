This week, I focused on the visual identity of the game and explored how different art directions could shape the same core gameplay. The mechanic is simple: players dig through a top-down surface to reveal paths, uncover objects, and collect pieces that contribute to a final mosaic. Because the gameplay remains constant, the main question is not what the game does, but what kind of world it lives in and how that world supports the overall experience.

I approached this by testing five possible themes for the same game. Each concept keeps the digging mechanic unchanged, while changing the atmosphere, characters, materials, and visual language. This helped me consider which direction would best balance readability, artistic personality, and production feasibility for a class project.

## Five Possible Art Directions

### 1. Underground Expedition

This is the most straightforward and recognizable concept. Players are tiny explorers moving through a subterranean world filled with dirt, rocks, minerals, and hidden treasure. The surface begins as brown earth, and as players dig, they reveal darker tunnels and bright collectible minerals.

- Universe: a playful underground mining world
- Characters: little miners with helmets, backpacks, and tools
- Collectibles: gems, mineral fragments, and colourful treasure blocks
- Mosaic goal: gather pieces to reconstruct a larger underground image

Pros:
- Fits the digging mechanic naturally
- Easy to understand immediately
- Simple 2D assets and tile-based terrain are achievable
- Allows for multiple character variations

Cons:
- Can feel generic if not designed carefully
- Brown terrain may become visually repetitive
- Requires a distinctive visual detail to make it memorable

### 2. Cosmic Excavation

This direction turns the game into an interplanetary mining adventure. Players explore an unknown planet beneath a strange, colourful surface, uncovering alien minerals and fragments of an ancient civilization. The world becomes more imaginative and futuristic, with unusual terrain, glowing materials, and astronaut-inspired characters.

- Universe: an unexplored alien planet
- Characters: small astronauts with helmets, suits, and gear
- Collectibles: glowing crystals and energy fragments
- Mosaic goal: reconstruct an ancient alien signal or image

Pros:
- Gives the game a strong identity
- Supports unusual colour palettes and environmental design
- Makes the characters easy to distinguish through costume and gear
- Creates a narrative layer for the mosaic objective

Cons:
- Space themes are common
- Excessive glow effects could be visually distracting
- Terrain and collectibles need strong contrast to remain readable

### 3. The World Inside a Painting

This is the strongest concept in terms of artistic connection. The game takes place inside an unfinished painting, where colour, brushstrokes, and pigment layers form the terrain. Players dig through the canvas to find missing pieces of colour and restore the artwork.

- Universe: a painted world existing inside a canvas
- Characters: paint creatures, brush characters, or artistic explorers
- Collectibles: pigment cubes, colour fragments, and paint pieces
- Mosaic goal: complete an unfinished artwork through the collected pieces

Pros:
- Strongest link between visual identity and the mosaic objective
- Brushstrokes and imperfect shapes add personality
- Does not require highly advanced illustration skills
- Gives the world a sense of progress as it becomes more colourful

Cons:
- Can become visually confusing if the terrain and collectibles are too similar
- Needs a consistent style to avoid clutter
- Surface textures must remain readable while still being expressive

### 4. The Mechanical Underground

This concept reimagines the game as an exploration inside a giant abandoned machine. Players dig through the layers of metal, pipes, gears, and machinery to recover coloured energy components and restore a central core.

- Universe: a giant mechanical system beneath the surface
- Characters: little mechanics, robots, or machine operators
- Collectibles: energy cubes and mechanical parts
- Mosaic goal: reconstruct a blueprint or energy core image

Pros:
- Highly recognizable visual identity
- Character design can remain simple yet expressive
- Combines organic dirt with mechanical objects in an interesting way
- The mosaic fits naturally into the machine’s core theme

Cons:
- Decorative detail can become too heavy
- Complex machinery may be difficult for future students to create consistently
- Needs a strong visual simplification to stay readable

### 5. The Forgotten Garden

This direction creates a whimsical underground garden filled with roots, mushrooms, plants, and hidden natural treasures. Beneath the soil lies a mysterious world that feels friendly, magical, and organic.

- Universe: a hidden garden beneath the earth
- Characters: gardeners, mushroom creatures, or forest explorers
- Collectibles: seeds, petals, stones, and natural fragments
- Mosaic goal: complete a large botanical image or creature

Pros:
- Very approachable and friendly atmosphere
- Plenty of opportunities for simple character design
- Natural objects create personality and charm
- Easy to make the world colourful and memorable

Cons:
- The digging mechanic needs to stay visually clear
- Plants and roots may obstruct the route or clutter the layout
- The relationship between the garden theme and the colour-collecting mechanic must be explained clearly

## Additional Research: Drawing Styles

In addition to the five themes, I also explored ten possible visual styles for the top-down digging game. These are important because the same gameplay can look very different depending on the drawing approach.

### 1. Pixel Art / Retro Game
- Visible square pixels and limited palettes
- Blocky characters and tile-based terrain
- Nostalgic arcade atmosphere
- Pros: easy to animate, consistent, and readable
- Cons: can become generic or time-consuming if over-detailed

### 2. Cozy Game / Cute Illustrated
- Rounded shapes and warm colours
- Friendly, expressive character design
- Soft environment details
- Pros: approachable and welcoming
- Cons: can feel cluttered if too many decorations are added

### 3. Flat Vector / Minimalist Cartoon
- Geometric shapes and solid colours
- Clean silhouettes and clear readability
- Pros: very easy to maintain consistency across a team
- Cons: can look plain without strong colours or character design

### 4. Hand-Drawn / Sketchbook
- Uneven outlines and imperfect shapes
- Playful, artistic style
- Pros: unique personality and expressive feel
- Cons: consistency can be difficult with multiple artists

### 5. Gouache / Painted Illustration
- Visible brushstrokes and textured colours
- Layered paint and pigment effects
- Pros: strong for an artistic project
- Cons: textures can become visually busy

### 6. Clay / Plasticine Style
- Soft, rounded shapes and handmade objects
- Charming and tactile look
- Pros: memorable and playful
- Cons: difficult to reproduce cleanly without looking messy

### 7. Paper Cut-Out / Collage
- Layered shapes made from coloured paper
- Handmade textures and visible geometic forms
- Pros: visually interesting and easy to stylize
- Cons: too many layers can reduce readability

### 8. Low-Resolution Retro / 8-Bit Inspired
- Simple shapes and limited colours
- Strong arcade identity
- Pros: nostalgic and easy to understand
- Cons: limited room for visual expression and character detail

### 9. Storybook / Fantasy Illustration
- Whimsical environments and imaginative forms
- Soft outlines and magical visual language
- Pros: great for playful worlds
- Cons: can become too detailed if the whole world is illustrated individually

### 10. Bold Graphic / Pop Art
- Saturated colours and strong outlines
- High contrast and energetic composition
- Pros: excellent visibility on projection screens
- Cons: can become overwhelming if every element competes for attention

## Suggested Combinations

Rather than choosing a theme and a style independently, I would test combinations like these:

- Underground Expedition + Cozy Pixel Art
- Cosmic Excavation + Flat Vector
- World Inside a Painting + Gouache
- Mechanical Underground + Paper Cut-Out
- Forgotten Garden + Storybook

These pairings would help the visual direction feel more intentional and cohesive, instead of mixing unrelated ideas.

## What I Would Prioritize for CART 415

My top three choices would be:

1. Flat vector / minimalist cartoon
2. Cozy pixel art
3. Paper cut-out or hand-drawn illustration

The reason is that these styles are readable, approachable, and easier to maintain across a group of students. Pixel art is not automatically the easiest choice, because it requires careful control of resolution, proportion, and animation. A flat vector or cozy style may actually be more practical for a project with a large team and a short production cycle.

## Conclusion

Overall, this week showed that the strength of the project depends just as much on the art direction as on the gameplay. The five concepts each offer a different way of framing the same digging mechanic, but the best direction is the one that connects the world, the objective, and the visual language in a clear and memorable way.

The strongest overall concept is The World Inside a Painting, because it naturally connects the underground digging mechanic with the mosaic objective and gives the project a distinctive artistic identity. For implementation, I would pair it with a simple but expressive style such as flat vector art or a soft cartoon look, which would keep the game readable, approachable, and feasible for a student team.

In the end, the goal is not simply to create a pretty game. It is to design a visual world that supports the interaction, strengthens the mood, and makes the player immediately understand the experience. This exploration provides a strong foundation for choosing a final direction that feels both creative and practical.
