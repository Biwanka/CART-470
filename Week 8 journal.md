I would approach it as five different art directions for the same game. The gameplay stays exactly the same, but the world, atmosphere, characters, materials and visual language change.

Since your game is top-down, with a solid surface that players dig through to create their own paths, I would explore these five concepts:
1. Underground Expedition
Most straightforward to develop










Concept: A playful underground exploration game. Players are little explorers searching for hidden treasures and colourful minerals beneath the earth.
- Universe: An underground mining world filled with dirt, rocks, minerals and hidden treasures.
- Environment: The entire screen starts as a brown dirt surface. Digging reveals darker tunnels and hidden colourful minerals.
- Characters: Simple little miners with different helmets, backpacks and tools.
- Collectibles: Colourful gems or mineral blocks.
- Mosaic: A large collection of minerals that players must bring together to create a final image.
Visual references to investigate:
- Terraria – underground exploration and digging.
- SteamWorld Dig – mining environment and character design.
- Motherload – resource collection and underground progression.
Pros
- Digging and collecting fit naturally into the universe.
- Easy to understand immediately.
- Simple 2D assets and tile-based terrain are achievable.
- Easy to create different character variations.
Cons
- Could look like a generic mining game.
- Brown terrain might make the environment monotonous.
- Needs a distinctive visual detail to connect it to the mosaic.
2. Cosmic Excavation
Most imaginative universe






















12











Concept: Players are astronauts exploring an unknown planet. Beneath its surface are strange minerals and fragments of an ancient alien civilization.
- Universe: An unexplored planet with mysterious underground resources.
- Environment: Purple, navy, blue or pink terrain, unusual rock formations and alien patterns.
- Characters: Small astronauts with different helmets, suits and backpacks.
- Collectibles: Glowing-looking crystals or alien energy fragments.
- Mosaic: An ancient alien image or signal that players must reconstruct.
Visual references to investigate:
- Spelunky – exploration and underground environments.
- Dome Keeper – resource collection and mining.
- Starbound – colourful alien worlds and exploration.
Pros
- Gives the game a strong identity.
- Allows unusual colour palettes and environmental designs.
- Astronaut characters are easy to distinguish through their equipment.
- The mosaic can become part of the game's story.
Cons
- Space themes are relatively common.
- Too many glowing effects could become distracting.
- The terrain and collectible blocks need strong contrast.
3. The World Inside a Painting
Strongest artistic connection






















8











Concept: Players are exploring an unfinished painting. The ground is made from layers of pigment, brushstrokes and colour. They dig through the canvas to find missing pieces of colour and help complete the artwork.
- Universe: An imaginary world existing inside a painting.
- Environment: Solid areas of colour, visible brushstrokes and layered textures. Digging reveals a different layer underneath.
- Characters: Little paint creatures, paintbrush characters or simple artistic explorers.
- Collectibles: Pigment cubes, colour fragments or little paint pieces.
- Mosaic: The final image is an unfinished artwork that players collectively complete.
Visual references to investigate:
- Chicory: A Colorful Tale – painting as a gameplay mechanic.
- Wilmot's Warehouse – simple shapes and colourful visual language.
- Monument Valley – stylized, graphic environments.
Pros
- The strongest connection between the visual identity and the mosaic objective.
- Imperfect shapes and brushstrokes can become part of the style.
- Does not require advanced illustration skills.
- Offers opportunities for the world to become more colourful as players progress.
Cons
- Could become visually confusing if the ground and collectibles look too similar.
- Needs a consistent illustration style.
- Textures should remain subtle enough to preserve readability.
4. The Mechanical Underground
Strong graphic identity






















11











Concept: Players explore the inside of a giant abandoned machine. They dig through its mechanical layers to recover missing coloured energy components and restore its central core.
- Universe: A giant underground mechanical system.
- Environment: Brown earth mixed with metal plates, copper pipes, gears and machinery.
- Characters: Small mechanics, robots or machine operators.
- Collectibles: Coloured energy cubes or mechanical components.
- Mosaic: The central image is a machine blueprint or energy core that must be reconstructed.
Visual references to investigate:
- SteamWorld Dig 2 – mechanical mining aesthetic.
- Machinarium – stylized mechanical character design.
- Wuppo – playful illustrated environments.
Pros
- Very recognizable visual identity.
- Character design can be simple but expressive.
- Interesting combination of organic dirt and mechanical objects.
- The mosaic can be integrated into the machine's central system.
Cons
- Could require too many decorative details.
- Complex machinery might be difficult for future students to create.
- Needs a simple visual language to avoid clutter.
5. The Forgotten Garden
Most playful and organic






















10











Concept: There is a hidden world beneath a garden. Players dig through the soil and uncover unusual plants, roots, mushrooms and colourful natural objects.
- Universe: A mysterious underground garden.
- Environment: Soil, large roots, mushrooms and underground plants.
- Characters: Little gardeners, mushroom creatures or forest explorers.
- Collectibles: Colourful seeds, petals, stones and natural fragments.
- Mosaic: A large botanical image, flower or imaginary creature.
Visual references to investigate:
- Garden Story – cute nature-inspired characters.
- A Short Hike – friendly illustrated world.
- Stardew Valley – pixel-art nature and environment.
Pros
- Very friendly and approachable atmosphere.
- Lots of possibilities for simple character designs.
- Natural elements can give the environment personality.
- Easy to create a colourful and recognizable world.
Cons
- The digging mechanic needs to be visually clear.
- Plants and roots could obstruct the tunnels.
- The relationship between the garden and collecting colour blocks needs explanation.

Here are 10 drawing styles worth exploring for your top-down digging game.
1. Pixel art / Retro game






















8











- Visible square pixels and limited colour palettes.
- Characters made from small pixel sprites.
- Repeating tile-based ground and tunnels.
- Retro arcade atmosphere.
Pros: Easy to animate, consistent, low-resolution assets and manageable for students.

Cons: Can look generic; detailed pixel art can actually be time-consuming.

References: Stardew Valley, Terraria, Eastward.
2. Cozy game / Cute illustrated






















11











- Rounded shapes and soft outlines.
- Warm, welcoming colours.
- Cute characters with expressive faces.
- Simple decorative details and friendly environments.
Pros: Very approachable for exhibition visitors, easy to give characters personality, and creates a welcoming atmosphere.

Cons: Can require consistent illustration skills; too many decorative details can clutter the screen.

References: Ooblets, Garden Story, A Short Hike.
3. Flat vector / Minimalist cartoon






















10











- Simple geometric shapes.
- Solid colours with minimal shading.
- Thick or consistent outlines.
- Very recognizable characters and objects.
Pros: Probably one of the easiest styles to produce consistently across a large student team. Very readable on a projected screen.

Cons: Can feel visually plain without a strong colour palette or distinctive shapes.

References: Mini Metro, Thomas Was Alone.
4. Hand-drawn / Sketchbook






















8











- Uneven outlines and imperfect shapes.
- Pencil, ink or marker textures.
- Handmade character designs.
- A playful illustrated feeling.
Pros: Unique personality, forgiving of small imperfections, and suitable for an artistic project.

Cons: Keeping different students' drawings consistent could be challenging.

References: Wilmot's Warehouse, Chicory: A Colorful Tale.
5. Gouache / Painted illustration






















8











- Visible brushstrokes and textured colour.
- Organic, imperfect shapes.
- Layered paint and pigment effects.
- More artistic and expressive than flat vector graphics.
Pros: Excellent for the painting universe; makes the game feel like an artwork.

Cons: Textures can become visually busy and require a consistent approach.

References: Chicory: A Colorful Tale, Gris.
6. Clay / Plasticine style






















12











- Rounded, soft-looking shapes.
- Objects appear handmade from clay.
- Slightly imperfect edges.
- Soft pastel or saturated colours.
Pros: Very recognizable identity; simple shapes can look charming and intentional.

Cons: Realistic clay textures and lighting can be difficult to reproduce. Better to imitate the style with flat illustrations than actual 3D models.
7. Paper cut-out / Collage






















12











- Shapes look cut from coloured paper.
- Flat layers with visible edges.
- Different textures and patterns.
- A handmade collage aesthetic.
Pros: Very interesting for an art-based game; simple shapes and textures are enough.

Cons: Too many overlapping layers may make the environment harder to read.
8. Low-resolution retro / 8-bit inspired






















12











- Very limited colours and detail.
- Blocky characters and terrain.
- Strong arcade feeling.
- Simple animations.
Pros: Easy to understand, nostalgic, and relatively simple to develop.

Cons: Less room for detailed character expression; can be visually restrictive.
9. Storybook / Fantasy illustration






















10











- Whimsical shapes and imaginative environments.
- Soft outlines and illustrated textures.
- A storybook or children's illustration atmosphere.
- More freedom in environmental design.
Pros: Great for creating a unique universe, especially the buried garden or alien planet.

Cons: Can become too detailed if the team tries to illustrate everything individually.
10. Bold graphic / Pop art






















10











- Strong outlines and saturated colours.
- High contrast.
- Repeated graphic patterns.
- Comic-book-inspired shapes and effects.
Pros: Excellent visibility on a large projection; colourful blocks and characters will stand out.

Cons: Can become visually overwhelming if every element competes for attention.

What I would prioritize for CART 415
My top three drawing styles would be:
1. Flat vector / Minimalist cartoon: easiest to maintain consistency across a large group of students.
2. Cozy pixel art: recognizable as a game, playful, and suitable for a top-down digging environment.
3. Paper cut-out or hand-drawn: gives the project a more artistic identity without requiring realistic 3D assets.
One important distinction: pixel art is not automatically the easiest option. Good pixel art requires careful control of resolution, proportions and animation. Flat vector graphics may actually be easier for students with different artistic abilities.
