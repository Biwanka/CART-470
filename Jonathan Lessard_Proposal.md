# Client : Jonathan Lessard / CART 

## A large crowd exhibition game 

### Context 
For the CART 415 game studio in winter, I’m considering designer and developing a classwide project destined to be showcase in the end of year exhibition. Inspired by Henry Smith’s “large crowd games” (couldn’t find proper online documentation, but shown at Ludodrome last year), I want the class to design a game projected on a large screen (a wall?) at which many visitors can play collectively with their phones: imagine scanning a QR code that leads to a simple Nintendo controller interface on the phone that controls an avatar on the collective screen amongst many others. 

### Design Problem  
• How to onboard visitors quickly and easily so that they can start playing ASAP and not spend 30 minutes configuring something. 

• What is the game? What do players do? Considering: 
- Probable latency in inputs/outputs 
- Player engagement relatively short 
- Players popping in and out continuously 

• What is the “stack”? 
- What’s the engine? On what hardware is it run? 
- What manages phone connections and input transmissions
- How to keep the whole thing relatively simple and manageable?

• What is the artistic direction? 
- Considering it has be implementable by a group of students with varying experience and skills.    

### Expected Outcome 
I am looking for propositions of different fidelity for all these questions, from “pitches”, to mockups, and ideally a “proof of concept” prototype that could be very minimal: people can connect with their phone to a link and press buttons, and see something happening one a shared screen (even just dots moving). The general idea is to start CART 415 with some significant foundations and be able to get moving relatively fast.
